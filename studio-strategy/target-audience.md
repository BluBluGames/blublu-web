# Target Audience / ICP

Status: Draft v4

## Working Notes
- Studio name: `blublu games`.
- Current evolution: started with 2D assets, now moving into 3D assets and custom commission work.
- Current business focus: commission work first, not just marketplace-only asset packs.
- Existing distribution: asset packs already published or queued on FAB, Unity Asset Store, itch.io, and GameDevFreelance.
- Market validation step added: separate assumptions from evidence before locking ICP.

## ICP Hypothesis
- Budget-qualified indie clients with a concrete game need and a clear brief.
- Small indie studios and funded indie teams are the strongest fit.
- Solo developers are only a fit when they have real budget, a clear scope, or outside funding.
- Clients preparing a demo, vertical slice, Kickstarter campaign, publisher pitch, or production sprint.
- Buyers making stylized, low-poly, or otherwise cohesive real-time worlds where consistency matters more than one-off asset hunting.

## Budget Bands
- Entry offer: $100+ for about 5 assets.
- Mid offer: roughly $600 for 24-35 assets.
- High offer: roughly $3200-$3500 for 140-200 assets.

## What Looks Real From Market Signals
- The market strongly rewards assets that are engine-ready, optimized, and easy to integrate.
- Buyers are often small teams or solo devs building prototypes, demos, game jams, mobile games, indie RPGs, or stylized worlds.
- Buyers respond to clear use cases like Unity, Unreal, Godot, Blender, VR/AR, and commercial-ready licensing.
- Commission demand is likely strongest where the buyer needs a cohesive environment or pack, not just a single decorative asset.
- "Open to everyone" is too broad for the homepage, but "open to any buyer with budget and a real brief" is a useful internal rule.

## Priority Buyer Ranking
1. Small indie studios with a budget and a defined production need.
2. Funded indie teams working toward a demo, publisher pitch, or vertical slice.
3. Solo developers only when they can show budget, clarity, and commitment.
4. Hobbyists or bargain-only buyers are low priority for commission work.

## Budget-Qualified Buyer Signals
- They can pay for a medium pack around $600 or a larger custom environment around $3200-$3500.
- They care about turnaround, coherence, and usability more than just the lowest possible price.
- They are likely comparing outsourcing against hiring a full-time artist or stitching together marketplace assets.
- They are most convincing when they have a demo, milestone, funding signal, or clear game direction.

## Search / AI Discovery Signals
- Prospects are more likely to find the studio through phrases like `custom 3D asset commissions`, `stylized low poly 3D assets`, `game-ready environment packs`, `indie game outsourcing`, `Unity 3D assets`, and `Unreal Engine environment art`.
- The homepage should say both what the studio makes and who it is for in explicit language, so search engines and AI tools can classify the business correctly.

## Validation Questions
- Are the main buyers hobbyists or serious indie teams with budget?
- Do they want per-asset work, full environment packs, or both?
- Is stylized low-poly the main buying trigger, or just one of several viable styles?
- What level of budget and turnaround do they actually expect?
- Which buyer types are least likely to convert and should be deprioritized?
